The Grammar of Solari

A handful of simple rules hold the whole language together.

Three ways to build a sentence

Saying what something is — like "he is my father": the word for "my" goes right before the noun, with no extra word for "is" needed. Shala talin lumana — he is my father.

Describing something — like "this is sacred": the subject, then the word for "is" (et), then the describing word. Dorn et zovar — this is sacred.

Doing something — like "he has a brother": always the action word first, then who's doing it, then what it's about. Iso shala betala — he has a brother.

Action words and time

The action word and the person doing it are always two separate words. A short word in front shows whether it already happened, is happening right now, or hasn't happened yet.

To see this in action with real examples, visit Word Construction.

Making a word plural, or showing that it belongs to someone

Solari adds a short ending to the word.

Plural:

veton (person) → vetonal (people)
The word ends in a consonant, so it adds -al.

lumana (father) → lumanal (fathers)
The word ends in a vowel, so it adds -l.

Belonging to someone:

veton (person) → vetonin (the person's)
The word ends in a consonant, so it adds -in.

lumana (father) → lumanan (the father's)
The word ends in a vowel, so it adds -n.

Plural endings: -al / -l  ·  Belonging endings: -in / -n

How new words get built

Solari builds most of its words by adding a short ending onto a simpler word, the way English turns "safe" into "safety." There are fourteen of these endings in total.

Visit Word Construction to browse every one of them with real examples.

A couple of exceptions worth knowing

Two words, sinat (stop) and chalat (follow), happen to end the way a certain word-building pattern would — but they aren't actually built that way. They're just their own whole words.

Four direction words blend together to make the in-between directions: lonor (north), lonev (south), lonir (east), lonux (west). This blending only happens with these four words.

lonor + lonir → norir (northeast)

lonor + lonux → norux (northwest)

lonev + lonir → nevir (southeast)

lonev + lonux → nevux (southwest)
