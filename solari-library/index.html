The Grammar of Solari

A handful of simple rules hold the whole language together.

Building a sentence

Solari has two kinds of sentence.

Saying what someone or something is. Start with the person or thing, then et (is), then what they are. Shala et talin lumana — he is my father. Dorn et zovar — this is sacred.

Saying what someone does. This is where Solari differs most from English. The action word comes first, then the person doing it, then what it is done to. Iso shala za betala — he has a brother. Word for word, it reads "has he a brother".

Past, present and future

The action word itself never changes. Instead, a short word goes in front of it to show when it happens.

Iso shala za betala — he has a brother. The present needs nothing extra.
Ik iso shala za betala — he had a brother. ik marks the past.
Un iso shala za betala — he will have a brother. un marks the future.

To show that something is still going on, add im straight after the time word. Ik im kreved shalal — they were fighting.

Two action words change their meaning with im. oltard means "know", but im oltard means "remember". spenot means "fear", but im spenot means "worry".

The same time words work with day and night: ik don yesterday  ·  un don tomorrow  ·  ik nod last night  ·  un nod tomorrow night

The, a and an

la means "the". za is used where English says "a". zaz is used where English says "an".

za wimvant — a house  ·  zaz vorima — an enemy  ·  la regasa — the king

Words like bread, water and peace take no article, just as in English. Ven tal krevos — I want peace.

More than one, or belonging to someone

Both are shown with a short ending. Which ending to use depends on the last letter of the word.

More than one. Add -al if the word ends in a consonant, or -l if it ends in a vowel.
veton (person) → vetonal (people)  ·  lumana (father) → lumanal (fathers)

Belonging to someone. Add -in if the word ends in a consonant, or -n if it ends in a vowel.
veton → vetonin (the person's)  ·  lumana → lumanan (the father's)

My, your, his and her. talin my  ·  temin your  ·  shalan his  ·  shalin her  ·  talalin our  ·  temalin your (more than one)  ·  shalalin their. These go before the word they belong to: talin lumani — my mother.

Describing words

A describing word goes before the thing it describes, as in English. za yoldiz motren — a big vehicle. za yurk vant — a new door.

tref (very) and kelvash (too, as in too much) go straight before the describing word. La purash et tref yentuz — the water is very cold.

Asking questions

Most question words begin with san-: sansak who  ·  sant what  ·  sanket why  ·  sanah when  ·  sannir where. The one exception is fesh, which means how.

Sannir im splet tem? — Where are you going?

Giving orders

To tell someone to do something, use the action word on its own, with no time word in front. You can add who it is for, or when, in the usual places.

Rulit! — Run!  ·  Rulit pumar! — Run now!  ·  Rulit tem! — You, run!
Donel la klis! — Give the key!  ·  Nopa rulit! — Don't run!

Any action word works this way: Nopa motrened! — Don't drive!  ·  Nopa zumek! — Don't jump!  ·  Sheled! — Fish!  ·  Fre pumar! — Flee now!

Yes, no and not

yah means yes and nop means no. To say that something is not so, use nopa. Like the time words, it goes in front of the action word, after ik or un.

Ik nopa wedak shala — he did not arrive.  ·  Nopa iso tal nesh — I do not have food.

Joining sentences together

ket and  ·  shen but  ·  pa or  ·  ravon because  ·  tozeb if  ·  vezon when  ·  narune while  ·  zenom although  ·  ordesh that, which or who

Each half of the sentence keeps its own normal order. Vezon ik wedak la krevtonal, ik fre la vetonal. — When the soldiers arrived, the people fled.

Saying where, how and with what

gom of  ·  tash from  ·  tu to  ·  rusha in  ·  runa out  ·  cul with  ·  culruna without  ·  thena on  ·  timas at  ·  akrot across  ·  viras between  ·  irama through  ·  faren for  ·  suren about

These come after the rest of the sentence, as in English. Ik kilt la voshakvetonal rusha la silven. — The rebels hid in the forest.

In and out have a small family of their own. rusha (in) gives rushak (to enter). runa (out) gives runak (to leave a place). This only happens with these two words.

How many and how much

meros many  ·  merot few  ·  zohos all  ·  zohot none  ·  zoh each  ·  solop some  ·  yana any  ·  enola enough  ·  onlay only

These go before the thing being counted. Iso la kranveton meros krevtonal — the general has many soldiers. Iso tal onlay grenu — I only have bread.

Can, still, already and yet

itlu can  ·  yulim still  ·  zumara already  ·  jas yet

Each one is always a separate word. Like nopa, they go in front of the action word, after ik or un.

Itlu kreved tal — I can fight.
Yulim im somnam la banal — the children are still sleeping.
Ik zumara wedam shalal — they already left.
Ik nopa jas wedak la regasa — the king has not arrived yet.

How new words get built

Most Solari words are made by adding a short ending to a simpler word, the way English turns "safe" into "safety". There are fifteen of these endings. One example: -em names an activity, so shel (fish) gives shelem (fishing).

Not every word ending in -el, -er or -en was built this way. faren (for) and suren (about) are whole words in their own right.

Joining two words into one

A few words are made by joining the first three letters of two other words. vrek (tree) and nesh (food) give vreknesh (fruit). teron (ground) and nesh give ternesh (vegetable).

Each of these was checked and agreed one at a time. It is how some of the language's words were made, not a way to invent new ones.

Sounds that belong to a family

Some openings are kept for one kind of word: dor- for this and that, san- for questions, et- for "to be" and sel- for how a person feels about themselves.

A plain -a or -i on the end of a people word shows whether the person is male or female: betala (brother), betali (sister). Apart from these, a root word or a describing word never ends in a plain -a or -i.

Counting

One to ten: ono, tol, zer, dis, velo, zek, sev, yul, sam, tir.

For eleven to nineteen, put tir (ten) in front: tir dis is fourteen. For the tens, join the number onto tir as one word: tirtol is twenty, up to tirsam for ninety. For hundreds, add ondar: zer ondar is three hundred. A thousand is tir ondar.

Bigger numbers are said hundreds first, then tens, then units: ono ondar tirvelo is 150. zer ondar tirvelo sev is 357. Like other amount words, a number goes before the thing being counted. Iso talal ono ondar tirvelo krevtonal — we have 150 soldiers.

Colours

There are three base colours: kav red  ·  zul blue  ·  fiv yellow

The colours in between are made by joining two of them: kavzul purple (red and blue)  ·  kavfiv orange (red and yellow)  ·  fivzul green (yellow and blue)

The rest are words of their own: novin white  ·  noduro black  ·  nodero grey  ·  bowen brown  ·  poy pink. nodesh means dark and donesh means light.

Colours work like any other describing word. La aveth et zul — the sky is blue. za kav vant — a red door.

A few exceptions worth knowing

sinat (stop) and chalat (follow) look as though they were built with an ending, but they weren't. They are whole words of their own.

burmar (bed) and shodar (bullet) end in -ar, but they are whole words too. They were not built with the -ar ending.

The four directions join to make the ones in between: lonor (north), lonev (south), lonir (east), lonux (west). The shared lon- is dropped when they join, which happens with no other words.

lonor + lonir → norir (northeast)
lonor + lonux → norux (northwest)
lonev + lonir → nevir (southeast)
lonev + lonux → nevux (southwest)
